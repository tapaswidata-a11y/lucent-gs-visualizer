The Indus (Sindhu) River System & The Panchnad

Bokhar Chu Glacier, The Five Punjab Rivers, and the Indus Waters Treaty (1960)

Main Stem

The Indus (Sindhu)

• Origin: Bokhar Chu glacier near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet (known as Singi Khamban / Lion's Mouth).
• Enters India in Ladakh, flowing northwest between the Ladakh and Zaskar ranges.
• Total length = 2,880 km (1,114 km in India).
• Receives Himalayan tributaries: Shyok (River of Death), Gilgit, Zaskar, Hunza, Shigar.

Panchnad Rivers

The Five Punjab Tributaries

• Jhelum (Vitasta): Verinag spring (Kashmir); forms boundary with Pakistan; flows through Wular Lake.
• Chenab (Asikni): Chandra + Bhaga at Tandi (HP); largest tributary of Indus.
• Ravi (Purushni): Originates near Rohtang Pass (HP).
• Beas (Vipasa): Originates at Beas Kund; lies entirely within India; joins Sutlej at Harike.
• Sutlej (Satadru): Rakas Lake (Tibet); antecedent river entering via Shipki La.

1960 Treaty

Indus Waters Treaty (IWT)

Signed on 19 September 1960 in Karachi by PM Jawaharlal Nehru and President Ayub Khan (brokered by the World Bank):
• Eastern Rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej): Allocated to India for unrestricted use.
• Western Rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab): Allocated to Pakistan, with India retaining non-consumptive usage rights for run-of-the-river hydroelectric power generation.

The Ganga River System & The Holy Panch Prayag

Headwaters of Alaknanda & Bhagirathi, The Five Confluences, and Major Tributaries

Devprayag, Uttarakhand • Birth of Ganga Zoom

Devprayag: Sacred confluence where Bhagirathi and Alaknanda unite to form the Ganga.

Course of the National River

  • Origin: Bhagirathi rises from Gangotri Glacier (Gaumukh, 3,900 m) in Uttarkashi. Alaknanda rises from Satopanth Glacier near Badrinath.
  • Plains Entry & Length: Enters the plains at Haridwar. Total length is 2,525 km (longest river in India), draining 8.6 lakh sq km across Uttarakhand, UP, Bihar, and West Bengal.
  • Right-Bank Tributaries:
    • Yamuna (1,376 km): Rises from Yamunotri glacier; joins Ganga at Prayagraj (Triveni Sangam). Fed by Chambal, Betwa, Ken, Sind.
    • Son: Rises at Amarkantak plateau (MP); flows north to join Ganga near Patna.
  • Left-Bank Tributaries: Ramganga, Gomti (originates at Fulhar Jheel / Gomat Taal, Pilibhit, UP), Ghaghara (largest tributary by water volume), Gandak, Kosi ('Sorrow of Bihar' due to shifting courses), Mahananda.
  • Delta & Sundarbans: Bifurcates at Farakka Barrage (WB) into Bhagirathi-Hooghly (flowing past Kolkata to the sea) and Padma (flowing into Bangladesh to join Jamuna and Meghna, forming the Sundarbans Mangrove Delta).

The Holy Panch Prayag (Essential Prelims Table)

Prayag (Confluence) Main River Tributary Joining District (Uttarakhand) Religious & Mythological Importance
1. Vishnuprayag Alaknanda Dhauliganga Chamoli First of the prayags down from Satopanth glacier; near Joshimath.
2. Nandaprayag Alaknanda Nandakini Chamoli Associated with King Nanda; holy sanctuary along the Badrinath pilgrimage route.
3. Karnaprayag Alaknanda Pindar River Chamoli Pindar originates from Pindari Glacier; site where Karna meditated in Mahabharata.
4. Rudraprayag Alaknanda Mandakini (Kali Ganga) Rudraprayag Mandakini flows down from Kedarnath glacier; site of severe 2013 flash floods.
5. Devprayag Alaknanda Bhagirathi Tehri Garhwal Most sacred confluence; the united stream is officially named The Ganga.

The Brahmaputra River System & Majuli Island

Yarlung Tsangpo, Namcha Barwa U-Turn, Braided Channels, and the World's Largest River Island

Brahmaputra River, Assam Zoom

Brahmaputra: Originates as Tsangpo in Tibet; enters India via the Great Bend of Namcha Barwa.

The Great Red River of Assam

  • Origin & Course: Rises from Chemayungdung glacier in the Kailash range near Mansarovar. Flows east for 1,100 km parallel to the Himalayas as the Yarlung Tsangpo ('The Purifier').
  • The Great Bend: Carves the Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon (world's deepest canyon) and makes an abrupt hairpin bend around Namcha Barwa (7,756 m), entering Arunachal Pradesh as Siang / Dihang.
  • Formation of Brahmaputra: At Sadiya (Assam), joined by the Dibang and Lohit rivers; thereafter officially called the Brahmaputra.
  • Tributaries:
    • Right-Bank: Subansiri (largest tributary, antecedent), Kameng (Jia Bhoreli), Manas, Sankosh, Tista.
    • Left-Bank: Burhi Dihing, Dhansiri, Kopili.
  • Majuli River Island: Enclosed between the main Brahmaputra and the Kherkutia Xuti channel, Majuli is recognized by Guinness as the world's largest inhabited freshwater river island and is India's first river island district.

Peninsular River Basins: East-Flowing vs. West-Flowing

Dakshin Ganga, Rift Valley Streams, Dhuandhar Falls, and the Tropic of Cancer Crossings

Dhuandhar Falls • Narmada River Zoom

Narmada: Longest west-flowing river of India, carving through the Marble Rocks at Bhedaghat.

The Great Peninsular Divide

  • East-Flowing Rivers (Bay of Bengal Drainage - form Deltas):
    • Godavari (1,465 km): Known as 'Dakshin Ganga' or 'Vridha Ganga'. Originates at Trimbakeshwar (Nashik, Maharashtra). Tributaries: Penganga, Wainganga, Wardha (unite as Pranhita), Indravati, Sabari, Manjira.
    • Krishna (1,400 km): Originates at Mahabaleshwar (Maharashtra). Tributaries: Tungabhadra, Bhima, Koyna, Musi (Hyderabad on its bank).
    • Cauvery (800 km): Known as 'Ganga of the South'. Originates at Talakaveri in Brahmagiri Hills (Coorg, Karnataka). Receives SW monsoon in summer and NE monsoon in winter (perennial flow). Site of Shivasamudram Falls.
    • Mahanadi (851 km): Originates in Raipur (Chhattisgarh); drains Odisha; site of Hirakud Dam.
  • West-Flowing Rivers (Arabian Sea Drainage - form Estuaries):
    • Narmada (1,312 km): Originates at Amarkantak (MP). Flows west through a structural Rift Valley between the Vindhyas and Satpuras. Site of Dhuandhar Falls and Sardar Sarovar Dam.
    • Tapti (724 km): Originates at Multai (Betul, MP); flows through rift valley south of Satpuras. Surat on its banks; Ukai Dam.
    • Mahi: Originates in Vindhyas (MP); unique river that crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice.
    • Luni (511 km): Originates in Aravallis (Pushkar); only major desert river; terminates in the Rann of Kutch (Inland drainage).
    • Sharavati (Karnataka): Site of Jog Falls (Gersoppa, 253 m).

Major Lakes & Multipurpose River Valley Projects

Wular to Chilika, Loktak's Floating Islands, and the Temples of Modern India

Famous Natural Lakes of India

  • • Wular Lake (J&K): Largest natural freshwater lake in India; tectonic origin; fed by the Jhelum River.
  • • Loktak Lake (Manipur): Largest freshwater lake in Northeast India; famous for circular floating vegetative masses called Phumdis and the Keibul Lamjao National Park (only floating national park in the world).
  • • Chilika Lake (Odisha): Largest brackish water coastal lagoon in India (Ramsar Wetland); famous for Irrawaddy dolphins.
  • • Pulicat Lake (Andhra/TN border): Second largest brackish water lake; separated from Bay of Bengal by the barrier island of Sriharikota (ISRO launch center).
  • • Sambhar Lake (Rajasthan): India's largest inland saline lake; major salt production center.
  • • Vembanad Lake (Kerala): Longest lake in India (96 km); site of Nehru Trophy Boat Race at Punnamada.
  • • Lonar Lake (Maharashtra): Famous crater lake in Buldhana district formed by a meteorite impact in basalt rock.

Multipurpose River Valley Projects

  • • Bhakra Nangal Project: Built on the Sutlej River in Himachal Pradesh/Punjab. One of the highest straight gravity dams in the world (226 m); reservoir named Gobind Sagar.
  • • Tehri Dam: Built on the Bhagirathi River in Uttarakhand. Highest dam in India (260.5 m); 8th tallest in the world.
  • • Hirakud Dam: Built on the Mahanadi River in Odisha. Longest earthen dam in the world (25.8 km total length).
  • • Sardar Sarovar Dam: Built on the Narmada River in Gujarat; provides irrigation and drinking water to drought-prone Saurashtra and Rajasthan.
  • • Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC, 1948): First multipurpose river valley project of independent India; modeled on the US Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA). Controls floods on the Damodar ('Sorrow of Bengal').
  • • Nagarjuna Sagar Dam: Built on the Krishna River on the Andhra Pradesh/Telangana border; one of the largest masonry dams.
