1. Fundamental Rights: The Magna Carta of India (Part III, Articles 12–35)

Justiciable political rights enforceable directly in the Supreme Court (Art 32) and High Courts (Art 226)

Lucent GK pp. 169–176
Articles 14–18 Category 1

Right to Equality

  • • Art. 14: Equality before law (UK) & Equal protection of the laws (USA). Rule of Law (Dicey).
  • • Art. 15: Prohibition of discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
  • • Art. 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment (Mandal case 1992, 50% reservation ceiling).
  • • Art. 17: Abolition of Untouchability (Civil Rights Protection Act 1955). Absolute right.
  • • Art. 18: Abolition of Titles (except military and academic distinctions).
Articles 19–22 Category 2

Right to Freedom

  • • Art. 19: 6 Freedoms: Speech & Expression, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession.
  • • Art. 20: Protection in respect of conviction: No ex-post facto law, no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination.
  • • Art. 21: Protection of Life & Personal Liberty. Maneka Gandhi (1978) 'Due Process'; Puttaswamy (2017) Right to Privacy.
  • • Art. 21A: Right to Education (6–14 yrs), added by 86th Amendment Act 2002.
  • • Art. 22: Protection against arrest & preventive detention (24-hour magistrate production).
Articles 23–24 Category 3

Right against Exploitation

  • • Art. 23: Prohibition of traffic in human beings, begar (forced unpaid labour), and bonded labour. Applies to both State and private citizens.
  • • Art. 24: Prohibition of employment of children below 14 years in any factory, mine, or other hazardous employment (Child Labour Prohibition Act).
Articles 25–28 Category 4

Freedom of Religion

  • • Art. 25: Freedom of conscience, free profession, practice, and propagation of religion (subject to public order, morality, health).
  • • Art. 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs and establish institutions.
  • • Art. 27: Freedom from taxes for promotion of any specific religion (Secular state).
  • • Art. 28: Freedom from religious instruction in wholly state-funded schools.
Articles 29–30 Category 5

Cultural & Educational Rights

  • • Art. 29: Protection of language, script, and distinct culture of minorities (and any section of citizens).
  • • Art. 30: Right of religious and linguistic minorities to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.
  • • Note: Art. 31 (Right to Property) repealed by 44th Amendment Act 1978; moved to Art. 300A.
Article 32 Heart & Soul

Right to Constitutional Remedies

Dr. Ambedkar proclaimed Article 32 as the single most critical article of the Constitution. It guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights via 5 Prerogative Writs.

2. The Five Prerogative Writs: SC (Art 32) vs HC (Art 226)

High-yield comparative matrix of writ types, legal translations, target authorities, and exceptions

Writs Matrix
Writ Name Literal Meaning (Latin) Target Authorities Grounds for Issuance Key Exceptions / Non-Applicability
1. Habeas Corpus "To have the body of" Issued against both Public and Private entities To produce a detained person before the court to test the legal validity of the detention; orders release if unlawful. Cannot be issued if detention is lawful, for contempt of legislature/court, or by competent court.
2. Mandamus "We Command" Public officials, government bodies, tribunals, inferior courts To compel a public authority to perform a mandatory statutory duty that it has refused to perform. Cannot be issued against private individuals, President of India, State Governors, or to enforce contract.
3. Prohibition "To Forbid" Lower Courts & Quasi-Judicial Tribunals Preventative writ issued to prevent an inferior court/tribunal from exceeding its legal jurisdiction or violating natural justice. Not available against administrative authorities, legislative bodies, or private individuals.
4. Certiorari "To be Certified" / "To be Informed" Lower courts, tribunals, and administrative authorities Both curative and preventive: quashes an illegal order already passed by lower tribunal for lack of jurisdiction or error of law on the face of record. Not available against legislative bodies or private individuals.
5. Quo-Warranto "By what authority or warrant?" Persons occupying a public office created by statute/Constitution Prevents illegal usurpation of a public office; inquires into legality of claim of a person to a public office. Special rule: Can be sought by ANY citizen without having personal interest (No locus standi needed).

3. Directive Principles of State Policy: Part IV (Articles 36–51)

Instrument of Instructions for establishing an egalitarian Welfare State and Socio-Economic Democracy

Part IV (DPSP)
Socialistic Welfare & Equity
• Art. 38: Promote welfare of the people; minimize inequalities in income, status, facilities.
• Art. 39(b): Distribution of material resources of the community to subserve common good.
• Art. 39(c): Prevention of concentration of wealth and means of production.
• Art. 39(d): Equal pay for equal work for both men and women.
• Art. 39A: Equal justice and Free Legal Aid to the poor (42nd Amend 1976 → NALSA Act 1987).
• Art. 41: Right to work, education, and public assistance in cases of unemployment, sickness.
• Art. 42: Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.
• Art. 43: Living wage, decent standard of life for workers.
Gandhian Grassroots & Swaraj
• Art. 40: Organization of Village Panchayats as units of self-government (led to 73rd Amend 1992).
• Art. 43: Promotion of cottage industries on an individual or co-operative basis in rural areas.
• Art. 43B: Promotion of voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, and professional management of Co-operative Societies (97th Amend 2011).
• Art. 46: Promotion of educational and economic interests of SCs, STs, and other weaker sections.
• Art. 47: Prohibition of the consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health.
• Art. 48: Prohibition of the slaughter of cows, calves, and other milch and draught cattle.
Liberal-Intellectual Modern State
• Art. 44: Secure a Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for citizens throughout the territory of India.
• Art. 45: Early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years (amended by 86th Amend 2002).
• Art. 48: Organization of agriculture and animal husbandry on modern and scientific lines.
• Art. 48A: Protection and improvement of environment, and safeguarding of forests and wildlife (42nd Amend 1976).
• Art. 49: Protection of monuments, places, and objects of artistic or historic interest.
• Art. 50: Separation of Judiciary from Executive in public services.
• Art. 51: Promotion of international peace and security, just and honourable relations between nations.

Balance between Fundamental Rights & DPSP (The Minerva Mills Doctrine)

In Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980), the Supreme Court held: "The Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Part III and Part IV. To give absolute primacy to one over the other is to disturb the harmony of the Constitution." Today, laws giving effect to Articles 39(b) and 39(c) are protected under Article 31C from challenge under Articles 14 and 19.

4. Fundamental Duties: Part IVA (Article 51A)

Recommended by the Swaran Singh Committee (1976), borrowed from USSR Constitution

11 Duties
(a) Abide by Constitution

To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.

(b) Cherish Freedom Ideals

To cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle for freedom.

(c) Protect Sovereignty & Unity

To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.

(d) Defend the Nation

To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so.

(e) Common Brotherhood & Women Dignity

To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all people of India and to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

(f) Value Composite Culture

To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture.

(g) Protect Natural Environment

To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

(h) Develop Scientific Temper

To develop the scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform.

(i) Safeguard Public Property

To safeguard public property and to abjure violence.

(j) Strive Towards Excellence

To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavor and achievement.

(k) Educational Opportunity for Children (Added by 86th Amendment Act, 2002)

Duty of a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years.
