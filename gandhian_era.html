Section 19 • Modern Indian History

The Gandhian Era: Mass Struggles & Independence (1919–1947)

The transformation of India’s freedom movement into an unstoppable mass upsurge under Mahatma Gandhi, the heroic martyrdom of Bhagat Singh, the march to Dandi, the thunderous war cry of Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose’s INA, and the dawn of freedom in August 1947.

01

Early Experiments: Champaran, Ahmedabad & Kheda

Return to India and the First Triad of Non-Violent Agitations

1917 • Bihar

Champaran Satyagraha

• Issue: European planters forced peasants to cultivate indigo on 3/20th of their land (Tinkathia System).
• Invitation: Raj Kumar Shukla persuaded Gandhi to visit Champaran.
• Milestone: Gandhi's First Civil Disobedience movement in India. An inquiry committee was appointed; planters refunded 25% of illegal exactions and Tinkathia was abolished.

1918 • Gujarat

Ahmedabad Mill Strike

• Issue: Mill owners wanted to discontinue the Plague Bonus, while workers demanded a 50% wage hike due to wartime inflation.
• Intervention: Gandhi supported workers (assisted by Anusuya Sarabhai) and undertook his First Hunger Strike in India.
• Outcome: Mill owners agreed to a 35% wage increase via a tribunal award.

1918 • Gujarat

Kheda Satyagraha

• Issue: Crops failed in Kheda district, but the colonial administration insisted on full land revenue extraction.
• Leadership: Gandhi along with Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and Indulal Yagnik organized peasants to withhold revenue.
• Milestone: Gandhi's First Non-Cooperation movement in India. Government secretly ordered revenue collected only from those who could pay.

02

Khilafat Movement & Non-Cooperation (1920–1922)

Hindu-Muslim Fraternity, Boycott of British Goods, and Chauri Chaura

The Khilafat Question & Launch

Following World War I, the British dismembered the Ottoman Empire and humiliated the Turkish Sultan (Caliph of Islam). In India, the Ali Brothers (Maulana Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali), Maulana Azad, and Hasrat Mohani formed the All India Khilafat Committee. Gandhi was elected President of the All India Khilafat Conference (Nov 1919).

The Non-Cooperation Movement was formally launched on 1 August 1920 (the day Bal Gangadhar Tilak passed away). The Tilak Swaraj Fund was oversubscribed with over ₹1 crore.

Nagpur Session & Chauri Chaura Withdrawal

At the Nagpur Session (Dec 1920) chaired by C. Vijayaraghavachariar, Congress endorsed NCM, reorganized Provincial Congress Committees on a linguistic basis, and admitted all adults paying 4 annas yearly membership fee.

Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922): At Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP), an angry crowd set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen. Shocked by the violence, Gandhi unilaterally called off the movement on 12 February 1922 through the Bardoli Resolution, leading to his arrest and 6-year sentence by Judge Broomfield.

03

Swaraj Party & The Revolutionary Martyrs (1923–1931)

Council Entry, Kakori Action, HSRA, and the Martyrdom of Bhagat Singh

Shaheed-e-Azam Bhagat Singh Zoom

Shaheed Bhagat Singh: "Inquilab Zindabad" (Long Live the Revolution).

Kakori Train Action 1925 Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan HRA held up 8-Down train near Lucknow. Bismil, Ashfaqullah, Roshan Singh, Rajendra Lahiri hanged.

04

Simon Commission (1927), Nehru Report & Lahore Purna Swaraj (1929)

The Challenge of Constitution-Making and the Unfurling of the Tricolor on River Ravi

1927–1928

Simon Commission Boycott

The British appointed an all-white 7-member Indian Statutory Commission under Sir John Simon without a single Indian member. Met everywhere with black flags and cries of 'Simon Go Back'. At Lahore, Lala Lajpat Rai was brutally lathi-charged by James A. Scott and died on 17 Nov 1928, declaring: 'Every blow on my body will prove a nail in the coffin of the British Empire.'

1928

The Nehru Report

In response to Secretary of State Lord Birkenhead’s arrogant challenge, an All Parties Conference appointed a committee under Motilal Nehru.
• Demanded Dominion Status, 19 Fundamental Rights (including adult suffrage & equal rights for women), and a Supreme Court.
• Rejected separate electorates in favor of joint electorates with reservation for minorities. Prompted Jinnah’s 14 Points (1929).

Dec 1929

Lahore Session & Purna Swaraj

Presided by Jawaharlal Nehru on the banks of river Ravi at Lahore.
• Passed the historic Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) resolution.
• On midnight of 31 December 1929, Nehru unfurled the Tricolor flag.
• Declared 26 January 1930 as the First Independence Day to be celebrated every year (commemorated later in 1950 as Republic Day).

05

The Salt Satyagraha & Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934)

The 240-Mile March to Dandi and the Pan-Indian Defiance of Colonial Law

Dandi Beach, Gujarat • April 1930 Zoom

Mahatma Gandhi at Dandi: Defiance of the British Salt Act.

The Dandi March & Pan-India Manifestations

  • The March: Gandhi set out from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930 with 78 chosen satyagrahis. Marched 240 miles (390 km) in 24 days to Dandi. On 6 April 1930, he picked up a handful of salt, breaking the law.
  • Tamil Nadu (Coromandel): C. Rajagopalachari led the march from Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam.
  • Malabar (Kerala): K. Kelappan marched from Calicut to Payyanur.
  • Dharasana Salt Works: Sarojini Naidu, Imam Saheb, and Manilal Gandhi led 2,500 peaceful marchers. American journalist Webb Miller documented the brutal police beatings that horrified world opinion.
  • North-West Frontier Province (NWFP): Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ('Frontier Gandhi') organized the non-violent Khudai Khidmatgars ('Servants of God', known as Red Shirts). Soldiers of the Garhwal Rifles (led by Chandra Singh Garhwali) refused to fire on unarmed Pathans at Peshawar.
  • North-East (Nagaland): 13-year-old Rani Gaidinliu raised the banner of revolt against British rule (honored as 'Rani' of the Nagas by Jawaharlal Nehru).
06

Round Table Conferences & The Poona Pact (1931–1932)

Gandhi-Irwin Talks, The Communal Award, and the Historic Agreement at Yerwada

Gandhi-Irwin Pact & 2nd RTC

  • • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931): Also known as the Delhi Pact. Viceroy Irwin agreed to release non-violent political prisoners and permit peaceful picketing; Congress agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and participate in the 2nd Round Table Conference.
  • • Karachi Session (March 1931): Chaired by Sardar Patel; endorsed the pact despite grief over Bhagat Singh's execution 6 days earlier. Passed landmark resolutions on Fundamental Rights and the National Economic Programme (drafted by Nehru).
  • • Second RTC (Sept–Dec 1931, London): Gandhi attended as the sole representative of Congress (alongside Sarojini Naidu, Malaviya). Talks deadlocked on minority electorates; Winston Churchill mocked Gandhi as a 'half-naked seditious fakir'.

Communal Award & Poona Pact (1932)

  • • Communal Award (16 August 1932): British PM Ramsay MacDonald announced separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans, and the Depressed Classes.
  • • Fast Unto Death: Gandhi saw separate electorates for Dalits as a colonial ploy to vivisect Hindu society and began a fast unto death in Yerwada Jail on 20 Sept 1932.
  • • Poona Pact (24 September 1932): Signed between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya (on behalf of Gandhi). Separate electorates for Depressed Classes were abandoned in favor of joint electorates with reserved seats increased from 71 to 147 in provincial legislatures and 18% in the Central Legislature.
07

Quit India Movement: The August Kranti (1942)

The 'Do or Die' Mantra, Operation Zero Hour, Underground Radio, and Parallel Governments

8 August 1942 • Bombay

The 'Do or Die' Mantra

Following the failure of the Cripps Mission (March 1942), the AICC met at Gowalia Tank Maidan (now August Kranti Maidan) in Bombay. Gandhi proclaimed the resolution demanding immediate British withdrawal from India and declared: 'Here is a mantra, a short one, that I give you... The mantra is: Do or Die (Karo ya Maro).'

9 August 1942 • Nationwide

Operation Zero Hour & Underground

Early morning on 9 August, British launched Operation Zero Hour, arresting Gandhi (imprisoned at Aga Khan Palace, Pune) and the entire Congress Working Committee (Ahmednagar Fort). Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the Indian flag at Gowalia Tank. Dr. Usha Mehta operated the secret underground Congress Radio, while JP Narayan and Ram Manohar Lohia mobilized youth from hiding.

Parallel Governments

Prati Sarkars of 1942

Local patriots ousted British authority and established independent parallel governments:
• Ballia (UP): Chittu Pandey established the first parallel government.
• Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal): Jatiya Sarkar with cyclone relief; 73-year-old Matangini Hazra was shot dead holding the Tricolor.
• Satara (Maharashtra): Prati Sarkar led by Nana Patil and Y.B. Chavan lasted the longest (until 1945).

08

Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose & The Azad Hind Fauj (INA)

The Military Offensive Against the Raj and the Great Red Fort Trials

Supreme Commander, Azad Hind Fauj Zoom

Netaji: "Give me blood and I shall give you freedom!"

The Sword of Indian Independence

  • Genesis of INA: Initially founded in 1942 by Captain Mohan Singh with Indian POWs captured by Japan in Malaya. Rash Behari Bose organized the Indian Independence League in Tokyo and handed over leadership to Subhash Chandra Bose in July 1943.
  • Provisional Government (21 Oct 1943): Netaji proclaimed the Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind (Provisional Government of Free India) in Singapore. Japan ceded the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, renamed Shaheed Dweep and Swaraj Dweep.
  • Regiments & Women's Wing: Formed Gandhi, Nehru, and Azad Brigades. Created the pioneering all-women Rani of Jhansi Regiment commanded by Captain Lakshmi Sahgal (Swaminathan).
  • The Battle for the Northeast: INA troops advanced to Kohima and Imphal alongside Japanese forces in 1944, hoisting the Tricolor on Indian soil at Moirang (Manipur) under Colonel Shaukat Malik.
  • Red Fort Trials (Nov 1945): British court-martial of three INA officers of different faiths—Colonel Prem Sahgal (Hindu), Colonel Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Sikh), and Major General Shah Nawaz Khan (Muslim)—at the Red Fort ignited massive public fury. Defended by Bhulabhai Desai, Tej Bahadur Sapru, and Jawaharlal Nehru, Commander-in-Chief Auchinleck was forced to remit their sentences.
09

Towards Independence: Cabinet Mission to August 1947

The RIN Mutiny, Interim Government, Mountbatten Plan, and The Dawn of Freedom

Mission / Event Date Key Personalities Lucent Prelims Key Takeaway
RIN Mutiny 18 Feb 1946 Naval ratings of HMIS Talwar (Bombay) Ratings struck work over racial discrimination and poor food. Surrendered on advice of Sardar Patel.
Cabinet Mission March 1946 Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander Rejected separate Pakistan. Proposed 3-tier federation (Groups A, B, C) and elected Constituent Assembly.
Direct Action Day 16 Aug 1946 M.A. Jinnah & Muslim League 'Great Calcutta Killings' unleashed by Muslim League government in Bengal to force partition.
Interim Government 2 Sept 1946 Jawaharlal Nehru (Vice-President) Formed without Muslim League initially; League joined in Oct 1946 (Liaquat Ali Khan as Finance Member).
Attlee's Declaration 20 Feb 1947 British PM Clement Attlee Announced British intention to transfer power to Indian hands by June 1948; replaced Wavell with Mountbatten.
Mountbatten Plan 3 June 1947 Lord Louis Mountbatten Plan for the partition of British India into two dominions: India and Pakistan. Accepted by Congress and League.
Independence of India 15 Aug 1947 Lord Mountbatten (1st GG) & Nehru (1st PM) Indian Independence Act 1947 (received Royal Assent on 18 July 1947). Sir Cyril Radcliffe drew boundary lines.
