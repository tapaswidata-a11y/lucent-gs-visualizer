Constitutional Acts Under East India Company (1773–1853)

The Progressive Subjection of Company Rule to Parliamentary Supervision

Act Key Constitutional Provisions Administrative & Legal Impact Lucent Prelims Key Fact
Regulating Act of 1773 Governor of Bengal elevated to Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings). 4-member council created. Madras and Bombay presidencies subordinated to Bengal in foreign and war matters. Established Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774); Sir Elijah Impey first Chief Justice.
Pitt's India Act of 1784 Introduced System of Dual Control: Court of Directors (commercial) and 6-member Board of Control (political/civil/military). Governor-General's Council reduced from 4 to 3 members. Company's territories named 'British possessions in India' for the first time.
Charter Act of 1793 Company's trade monopoly renewed for 20 years. Home government members paid out of Indian revenues. Overriding powers of Governor-General over his council codified. Began the financial burden on Indian exchequer that Naoroji analyzed as 'Drain'.
Charter Act of 1813 Abolished Company's trade monopoly in India; preserved monopoly only in tea trade and trade with China. Christian missionaries permitted to preach in India. Allotted ₹1 Lakh annually for education and science promotion in India.
Charter Act of 1833 Governor-General of Bengal redesignated as Governor-General of India (Lord William Bentinck). Completely ended commercial role of EIC. Stripped Bombay and Madras of law-making powers; centralized all legislation. Added 4th member (Law Member: Lord Macaulay) and established 1st Law Commission.
Charter Act of 1853 Separated for the first time the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General’s Council (6 legislative councilors added). Local representation introduced in the Central Legislative Council. Introduced open competitive examination for Civil Services (ICS) (Macaulay Committee 1854).
02

Constitutional Acts Under The British Crown (1858–1947)

Direct Imperial Governance, Communal Electorates, Dyarchy, and Provincial Autonomy

Act Key Constitutional Provisions Representative & Legislative Changes Lucent Prelims Key Fact
GoI Act 1858 (Good Government Act) Dissolved East India Company; power transferred to British Crown. Abolished Board of Control and Court of Directors. Created Secretary of State for India (Cabinet minister) assisted by 15-member Council of India. Governor-General given title of Viceroy of India (Lord Canning first).
Indian Councils Act 1861 Introduced Portfolio System (Canning). Restored legislative powers to Bombay & Madras (decentralization). Viceroy nominated non-official Indians: Raja of Banaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao. Viceroy empowered to issue Ordinances valid for 6 months without council assent.
Indian Councils Act 1892 Introduced principle of indirect election (recommendation by universities, district boards, chambers of commerce). Legislative council members given right to discuss annual budget and ask questions on public interest (6 days notice). No power to vote on budget or ask supplementary questions.
Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto) Introduced Separate Communal Electorates for Muslims. Lord Minto called 'Father of Communal Electorate'. Non-official majority allowed in provincial councils. Right to ask supplementary questions and move budget resolutions. S.P. Sinha became 1st Indian member in Viceroy's Executive Council (Law Member).
GoI Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford) Introduced Dyarchy in Provinces (Reserved Subjects under Governor; Transferred Subjects under Ministers). Introduced Bicameralism and direct elections at the Centre. Extended separate electorates to Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians. Provided for establishment of a Public Service Commission (set up in 1926).
GoI Act 1935 Proposed All-India Federation (provinces + princely states). Abolished Dyarchy in provinces and granted Provincial Autonomy. Dyarchy introduced at Centre (Foreign Affairs, Defence). Created 3 Lists: Federal, Provincial, Concurrent. Established Federal Court (1937) and Reserve Bank of India (1935). Burma separated.
Indian Independence Act 1947 Ended British rule; declared India an independent sovereign state from 15 August 1947. Created two independent dominions: India and Pakistan. Lapsed British paramountcy over princely states. Constituent Assemblies of both dominions empowered to frame constitutions and repeal British acts.

Key Governors-General: Bentinck & Dalhousie

The Social Reformer and the Imperial Architect of Modern Indian Infrastructure

Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835) Zoom

Bentinck’s Historic Legacy

  • • Abolition of Sati (1829): Enacted Regulation XVII declaring Sati illegal and punishable as culpable homicide (collaborated with Raja Ram Mohan Roy).
  • • Suppression of Thuggee (1830): Appointed Captain William Sleeman who eradicated the criminal cult of Thugs across central India.
  • • English Education Act (1835): Adopted Macaulay’s Minute; made English the official medium of higher education and administration.
  • • Calcutta Medical College (1835): Founded the first Western medical institution in Asia.
Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) Zoom

Dalhousie’s Double-Edged Sword

  • • Doctrine of Lapse: Annexed Satara (1848), Jaitpur & Sambalpur (1849), Baghat (1850), Udaipur (1852), Jhansi (1853), and Nagpur (1854).
  • • Annexation of Awadh (1856): Deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah on pretext of 'misgovernance' based on Outram's report (primary cause of 1857 revolt).
  • • Indian Railways (1853): 1st passenger train ran from Bombay (Bori Bunder) to Thane on 16 April 1853 (34 km).
  • • Telegraph & Post: 1st telegraph line between Calcutta and Diamond Harbour / Agra (1853); modern Postal Act of 1854 with uniform 2-paisa postage.
  • • Wood’s Despatch (1854): Known as the 'Magna Carta of English Education in India'; Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856).

Master Chronological Table: British Viceroys of India (1858–1947)

Complete Reference Matrix for APSC, UPSC, and State Civil Services Prelims

Viceroy Tenure Major Historic Events & Administrative Highlights
Lord Canning 1856–1862 1857 Revolt; Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858); GoI Act 1858; Indian Penal Code (1860); Indian Councils Act 1861; High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1862).
Lord Elgin I 1862–1863 Wahabi Movement suppressed. Died in Dharamshala, Himachal Pradesh.
Lord Lawrence 1864–1869 Bhutan War (1865); Policy of 'Masterly Inactivity' towards Afghanistan; Telegraphic communication opened with Europe (1865).
Lord Mayo 1869–1872 Financial decentralization (1870); Department of Agriculture created; Mayo College at Ajmer; First Census of India (1872); Assassinated in Andamans by Sher Ali.
Lord Northbrook 1872–1876 Kuka Movement in Punjab led by Ram Singh; Prince of Wales (later Edward VII) visited India (1875); Bihar famine (1873–74).
Lord Lytton 1876–1880 'Viceroy of Reverse Genius'; Grand Delhi Durbar 1877 (Queen Victoria as Kaiser-i-Hind amidst Great Famine); Vernacular Press Act (1878); Arms Act (1878); Lowered ICS age limit from 21 to 19; 2nd Anglo-Afghan War.
Lord Ripon 1880–1884 'Father of Local Self-Government' (1882); Repealed Vernacular Press Act (1882); 1st Synchronous Census (1881); 1st Factory Act (1881); Hunter Commission on Education (1882); Ilbert Bill controversy (1883).
Lord Dufferin 1884–1888 Foundation of Indian National Congress (1885); 3rd Anglo-Burmese War (annexation of Upper Burma, 1885); Bengal Tenancy Act (1885).
Lord Lansdowne 1888–1894 2nd Factory Act (1891); Indian Councils Act 1892 (indirect election introduced); Durand Line demarcated between British India and Afghanistan (1893).
Lord Elgin II 1894–1899 Chapekar brothers assassinated plague commissioner Rand in Poona (1897); Great Famine (Lyall Commission); Birsa Munda revolt.
Lord Curzon 1899–1905 Police Commission (Andrew Frazer); Indian Universities Act (1904); Ancient Monuments Preservation Act (1904); Younghusband Expedition to Tibet (1904); Partition of Bengal (1905).
Lord Minto II 1905–1910 Foundation of Muslim League (1906); Surat Split in INC (1907); Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) with separate communal electorates for Muslims.
Lord Hardinge II 1910–1916 Delhi Durbar 1911 (Bengal partition annulled, capital transferred to Delhi); Bomb thrown at him by Rash Behari Bose (1912); Gandhi returned to India (1915); Hindu Mahasabha founded (1915).
Lord Chelmsford 1916–1921 Lucknow Pact (1916); Home Rule Leagues (1916); August Declaration (1917); Rowlatt Act (1919); Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919); GoI Act 1919; Non-Cooperation Movement launched (1920).
Lord Reading 1921–1926 Chauri Chaura incident (1922); Swaraj Party founded (1923); Kakori Train Dacoity (1925); RSS founded (1925); Moplah Rebellion in Kerala (1921). Only Jewish Viceroy of India.
Lord Irwin 1926–1931 Simon Commission (1927); Nehru Report (1928); Lahore Session Purna Swaraj (1929); Dandi March & Civil Disobedience (1930); 1st RTC (1930); Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931).
Lord Willingdon 1931–1936 2nd RTC (1931) & 3rd RTC (1932); Communal Award (1932); Poona Pact (1932); Government of India Act 1935; Reserve Bank of India established (1935).
Lord Linlithgow 1936–1944 1937 Provincial elections; World War II broke out (1939); August Offer (1940); Cripps Mission (1942); Quit India Movement (1942); Great Bengal Famine (1943). Longest tenure as Viceroy.
Lord Wavell 1944–1947 Wavell Plan & Shimla Conference (1945); INA Trials (1945); RIN Mutiny (1946); Cabinet Mission Plan (1946); Interim Government formed; Direct Action Day (1946).
Lord Mountbatten 1947–1948 Last Viceroy of British India and First Governor-General of Free India; June 3rd Plan (Mountbatten Plan); Indian Independence Act 1947; Partition into India & Pakistan.
C. Rajagopalachari 1948–1950 The first and only Indian Governor-General of India until the Constitution came into effect on 26 January 1950 and the office was abolished.
