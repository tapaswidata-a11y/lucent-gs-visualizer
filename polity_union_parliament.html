1. The President & Vice-President of India (Articles 52–73)

De jure Head of State, Supreme Commander of Armed Forces, and First Citizen of India

Lucent GK pp. 177–185
Click to Zoom (Full Detail)

Samvidhan Sadan (The Historic Parliament of India, New Delhi)

Under Article 79, Parliament consists of the President of India and the two Houses: the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and the House of the People (Lok Sabha).

Electoral College (Art 54 & 55)

Elected by proportional representation (Single Transferable Vote). College consists of:
• Elected MPs of Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha.
• Elected MLAs of all State Assemblies.
• Elected MLAs of Delhi & Puducherry (70th Amend).
Nominated MPs/MLAs and State MLCs DO NOT vote!

Impeachment (Art 61)

Sole ground: "Violation of the Constitution". Quasi-judicial process:
• 14 days notice signed by 1/4th members.
• Passed by not less than 2/3rd of TOTAL membership of that House.
• Passed by 2/3rd total membership of the other House.
Nominated MPs CAN vote in impeachment!

Ordinances (Art 123)

Promulgated when either or both Houses are not in session and circumstances require immediate action. Same legal force as an Act of Parliament. Must be laid before Parliament and expires 6 weeks from reassembly. Max lifespan is 6 months + 6 weeks. Subject to judicial review (D.C. Wadhwa case).

Pardoning Powers (Art 72)

• Pardon: Completely absolves guilt & punishment.
• Commutation: Substitution to a lighter punishment.
• Remission: Reducing period without changing character.
• Respite: Lesser sentence due to special fact (pregnancy).
• Reprieve: Temporary stay of execution (death penalty).

2. The Prime Minister & Council of Ministers (Articles 74, 75, 78)

De facto real executive, 'primus inter pares' (first among equals), and keystone of the Cabinet arch

Executive Realities

Aid & Advice of Cabinet (Art 74)

There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President. The 42nd Amendment (1976) made the advice binding. The 44th Amendment (1978) gave the President the power to return the advice once for reconsideration; but if sent back, the President MUST accept it.

Collective Responsibility (Art 75(3))

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. They sink or swim together. If a No-Confidence Motion is passed in the Lok Sabha, all ministers, including those from Rajya Sabha, must resign. Ministers are also individually responsible to the President (pleasure of President per Art 75(2)).

15% Cabinet Size Ceiling (91st Amend)

To stop jumbo cabinets and opportunistic defections, the 91st Constitutional Amendment Act 2003 enacted Article 75(1A): The total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Union Council of Ministers shall NOT exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha. In States (Art 164(1A)), max 15%, min 12 ministers.

3. The Parliament: Lok Sabha vs. Rajya Sabha Powers

Bicameral legislature under Article 79: Composition, tenure, special powers, and qualifications

Bicameralism
Feature Lok Sabha (House of the People - Art 81) Rajya Sabha (Council of States - Art 80)
Chamber Status Lower House / Popular Chamber (directly elected by citizens) Upper House / House of Elders (indirectly elected by State MLAs)
Maximum Strength 550 Members (530 from States + 20 from UTs). 104th Amend 2019 ended 2 Anglo-Indian nominations. 250 Members (238 elected from States/UTs + 12 nominated by President in Literature, Science, Art, Social Service).
Tenure & Dissolution Normal term: 5 years. Subject to dissolution at any time by President. Can be extended by 1 year during National Emergency. Permanent body; NOT subject to dissolution. 1/3rd of members retire every 2nd year. Individual member term is 6 years.
Minimum Age 25 Years 30 Years
Presiding Officer Speaker (elected from amongst Lok Sabha members) and Deputy Speaker Vice-President of India (Ex-officio Chairman per Art 64) and Deputy Chairman (elected from RS members)
Special Exclusive Powers • Money Bills can originate ONLY in Lok Sabha (Art 109).
• Speaker presides over Joint Sittings (Art 108).
• Only Lok Sabha can pass a No-Confidence Motion to dismiss the Government. 		• Article 249: Authorize Parliament to legislate on State List for 1 year in national interest (2/3rd vote).
• Article 312: Create new All-India Services (2/3rd vote).
• Removal of Vice-President originates ONLY in RS.

4. Legislative Procedures, Parliamentary Devices & Standing Committees

Money Bills (Art 110), Joint Sittings (Art 108), Question Hour, Zero Hour, and the Public Accounts Committee

Procedures
22 Members 15 LS + 7 RS

Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

Oldest financial committee (set up 1921). Examines the annual audit reports of the Comptroller & Auditor General of India (CAG). The CAG acts as its "guide, philosopher, and friend". By convention since 1967, its Chairman is appointed by the Speaker from the Opposition party.

30 Members 100% Lok Sabha

Estimates Committee

Largest Parliamentary Committee (established 1950 on John Mathai's suggestion). Examines budget estimates to suggest "economies, organizational improvements, and administrative reforms". Known as the 'Continuous Economy Committee'. Rajya Sabha has zero representation!

22 Members 15 LS + 7 RS

Committee on Public Undertakings

Created in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna Menon Committee. Examines the reports, accounts, and commercial efficiency of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) like ONGC, SAIL, IOCL, and CAG commercial audit reports. Chairman is appointed by Speaker from Lok Sabha members only.

Money Bill (Article 110) Specific Provisions

• Contains exclusively matters listed in Art 110: Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any tax; borrowing of money; custody of Consolidated Fund of India.
• Introduced ONLY in Lok Sabha with the prior recommendation of the President.
• Speaker's endorsement as Money Bill is final and non-justiciable.
• Rajya Sabha has only 14 days to return it with or without recommendations. Cannot amend or reject.
• NO Joint Sitting is permitted under Article 108 for a Money Bill!
