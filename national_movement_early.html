Foundation of the Indian National Congress (1885)

Genesis, Pioneers, Theories of Origin, and Milestone Annual Sessions

Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay Zoom

First Session of the Indian National Congress (28 December 1885). Presided by Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee.

Genesis and Early Modalities

  • Originator: Allan Octavian Hume (A.O. Hume), a retired Scottish civil servant, organized the initial gathering with the blessings of Viceroy Lord Dufferin.
  • Date & Venue: Originally planned in Pune, but shifted to Bombay due to a cholera outbreak. Convened on 28 December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College with 72 delegates.
  • First President: Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee), a prominent barrister from Calcutta.
  • Safety Valve Theory vs. Lightning Conductor: Lala Lajpat Rai (*Young India*) advanced the 'Safety Valve Theory' claiming Hume formed Congress to release brewing anti-British discontent. Modern historians (like Bipan Chandra) describe Hume as a 'Lightning Conductor' utilized by Indian nationalists to build an all-India political platform without immediate colonial suppression.

The Ideological Divide: Moderates vs. Extremists

Evolution from Constitutional Mendicancy to Militant Nationalism

Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak Zoom

Bal Gangadhar Tilak: "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it."

Parameter Moderate Phase (1885–1905) Extremist Phase (1905–1919)
Prominent Leaders Dadabhai Naoroji, G.K. Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, S.N. Banerjee, Dinshaw Wacha. Lal-Bal-Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, B.G. Tilak, B.C. Pal) & Sri Aurobindo Ghosh.
Ultimate Goal Self-government within the British Empire (Dominion status) & administrative reforms. Complete Independence / Swaraj without foreign control.
Methodology '3 Ps': Prayer, Petition, and Peaceful Protest within British law. Direct Action: Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education, Passive Resistance.
Belief in British Justice Total faith in British sense of fair play and providential rule. Rejected British benevolent rule; believed self-sacrifice alone brings liberty.
Social Base Narrow base: English-educated urban elite, lawyers, doctors, zamindars. Broader base: Educated youth, urban middle class, lower-middle class, and workers.
Cultural Inspiration Western liberal thought (Mill, Burke, Spencer, Gladstone). Indian cultural heritage, Gita, Shivaji, and Vedic glory.
03

Partition of Bengal (1905) & Swadeshi Movement

Curzon's Imperial Strike and the Spark of Nationwide Boycott

1

The Partition Scheme

Announced by Lord Curzon on 20 July 1905; came into effect on 16 October 1905. Bengal was divided into:
• Bengal: West Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa (Capital: Calcutta).
• Eastern Bengal & Assam: Capital at Dacca (Lieutenant Governor: Bampfylde Fuller).
Real objective: To crush the nerve center of Indian nationalism and create a Muslim-majority province to foster communal divide.

2

Resistance & Day of Mourning

16 October 1905 was observed throughout Bengal as a Day of Mourning. People fasted, tied Rakhi on each other’s wrists as a symbol of brotherhood, and marched barefoot to the Ganges singing Vande Mataram. Rabindranath Tagore composed the anthem 'Amar Sonar Bangla' (later national anthem of Bangladesh).

3

Swadeshi Institutions

The movement fostered self-reliance (Atmashakti):
• National Council of Education established on 15 August 1906; Bengal National College opened with Aurobindo Ghosh as principal.
• P.C. Ray founded the famous Bengal Chemical Swadeshi Stores.
• Swadesh Bandhab Samiti founded by Ashwini Kumar Dutt mobilized thousands in Barisal.

04

Muslim League (1906), Surat Split (1907) & Morley-Minto (1909)

Communal Electorates, Internal Ruptures, and Imperial Concessions

1906 • Dacca

Foundation of Muslim League

Following the Shimla Deputation led by the Aga Khan meeting Lord Minto in October 1906, the All India Muslim League was formally founded at Dacca on 30 December 1906.
• Founders: Nawab Salimullah of Dacca, Nawab Mohsin-ul-Mulk, Nawab Waqar-ul-Mulk.
• First President: Aga Khan.
• Supported Bengal partition and demanded separate electorates.

1907 • Surat

The Surat Split

The INC session held on the banks of Tapti river at Surat in December 1907 witnessed open physical violence.
• Presided over by Rash Behari Ghosh.
• Dispute over extending boycott to all British institutions and electing Tilak or Lajpat Rai as president.
• Extremists were expelled from Congress; Tilak was imprisoned in Mandalay (Burma) for 6 years (1908–1914) where he wrote Gita Rahasya.

1909 • London/Delhi

Morley-Minto Reforms

Officially the Indian Councils Act 1909:
• Introduced Separate Electorates for Muslims, sowing institutional seeds of communalism. Lord Minto was named 'Father of Communal Electorate'.
• Expanded Imperial Legislative Council seats from 16 to 60.
• Satyendra Prasad Sinha (S.P. Sinha) became the first Indian appointed to the Viceroy's Executive Council (as Law Member).

Home Rule Movement & The Lucknow Pact (1916)

The Twin Leagues of Tilak and Besant & The Triumph of Nationalist Unity

Tilak’s Home Rule League

April 1916 • Poona
  • • Headquarters: Poona (Belgaum conference).
  • • Jurisdiction: Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, and Berar (6 branches total).
  • • Organs: Kesari (Marathi) and The Mahratta (English).
  • • Demands: Swarajya, formation of linguistic states, and education in the vernacular tongue.

Annie Besant’s Home Rule League

Sept 1916 • Madras
  • • Headquarters: Adyar (near Madras). Key associate: George Arundale (Organizing Secretary).
  • • Jurisdiction: Bombay city and the rest of India (around 200 branches).
  • • Organs: New India and Commonweal.
  • • Milestone: Besant was arrested in June 1917, triggering nationwide protests; Sir S. Subramania Aiyar renounced his knighthood. Besant was elected INC President in December 1917.

The Historic Lucknow Session (December 1916)

Presided over by Ambica Charan Mazumdar, the 1916 Lucknow Session marked two decisive breakthroughs in Indian history:

1. Moderate–Extremist Reunion

After 9 years of bitter estrangement since the 1907 Surat split, Tilak and Annie Besant successfully negotiated the readmission of the Extremists into the Congress fold.

2. Congress–League Lucknow Pact

Congress and Muslim League presented joint demands to the British government. Congress made the momentous concession of accepting separate communal electorates for Muslims in provincial legislatures.

07

The Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)

The Infamous 'Black Act', Amritsar's Bloody Baisakhi, and the Hunter Commission

Flame of Liberty Memorial, Amritsar Zoom

Jallianwala Bagh Memorial, Amritsar: 13 April 1919 tragedy that alienated India forever.

The Sequence of Imperial Terror

  • The Rowlatt Act (March 1919): Recommended by Sir Sidney Rowlatt (Sedition Committee). Empowered the British government to imprison any person suspected of terrorism for up to 2 years without trial. Condemned across India as 'No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal'. Gandhi founded the Satyagraha Sabha to protest.
  • Arrest of Popular Leaders: On 9 April 1919, two beloved nationalist leaders of Punjab, Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal, were arrested in Amritsar.
  • The Massacre (13 April 1919): On Baisakhi day, thousands of villagers, women, and children gathered peacefully in Jallianwala Bagh. Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer sealed the single narrow exit with British and Gurkha troops and ordered firing without warning until ammunition was exhausted (1,650 rounds fired).
  • National Renunciations & Retribution:
    • Rabindranath Tagore surrendered his British Knighthood.
    • Mahatma Gandhi returned his Kaiser-i-Hind gold medal.
    • Hunter Committee (Disorders Inquiry Committee) was appointed to whitewash the incident.
    • On 13 March 1940, revolutionary Sardar Udham Singh assassinated Sir Michael O'Dwyer (then Lieutenant Governor of Punjab who endorsed the shooting) at Caxton Hall, London.
