Classification of Rocks & The Rock Cycle

Igneous, Sedimentary, and Metamorphic Rocks with Metamorphic Transformations

Primary Rocks

Igneous Rocks

Formed by cooling and crystallization of molten magma/lava. Non-stratified, crystalline, devoid of fossils.
• Plutonic (Intrusive): Cools slowly deep underground with large crystals (e.g., Granite, Gabbro, Diorite).
• Volcanic (Extrusive): Cools rapidly on the surface with fine grain (e.g., Basalt of Deccan Traps, Pumice, Obsidian).

Stratified Rocks

Sedimentary Rocks

Formed by lithification and compaction of layered sediments. Contain fossils and source of petroleum/coal.
• Mechanically Formed: Sandstone, Shale, Conglomerate, Loess.
• Organically Formed: Limestone, Coal, Peat, Chalk.
• Chemically Formed: Rock Salt, Gypsum, Potash.

Changed Rocks

Metamorphic Rocks

Formed when pre-existing rocks undergo mineral recrystallization under extreme heat (thermal) and pressure (dynamic).
• Highly compact and foliate.
• Examples: Slate, Schist, Gneiss, Quartzite, Marble, Anthracite.

Earthquakes & Volcanoes: The Dynamic Earth

Seismic Body & Surface Waves, The Richter Scale, and Stromboli's Eternal Fires

Stromboli, Italy • Continuous Eruption Zoom

Stromboli: 'Lighthouse of the Mediterranean' (Active composite volcano).

Seismic Anatomy of Earthquakes

  • Focus (Hypocenter) vs. Epicenter: Focus is the subsurface point where seismic energy is first released. The Epicenter is the point directly vertical on Earth's surface above the focus (experiences the first and strongest shock).
  • P-Waves (Primary): Longitudinal waves (push-pull); fastest (6–13 km/s); travel through solids, liquids, and gases.
  • S-Waves (Secondary): Transverse shear waves (up-and-down); slower; travel only through solids. Completely blocked by Earth's liquid outer core, creating an S-wave shadow zone beyond 105° from epicenter.
  • L-Waves (Surface Waves): Slowest; travel along surface crust; cause the greatest ground displacement and structural damage.
  • Scales of Measurement:
    • Richter Scale: Measures energy magnitude logarithmically (each unit represents a 31.6-fold increase in energy release). Scale 0–9+.
    • Mercalli Scale: Measures visible intensity / damage (scale I to XII).
  • Pacific Ring of Fire: Circum-Pacific belt where tectonic subduction zones host over 75% of the world's active volcanoes and 90% of all earthquakes.

Structure & Stratification of the Atmosphere

From the Weather-Riddled Troposphere to the Radio-Reflective Ionosphere

Atmospheric Layers & Thermal Gradients Zoom

Thermal layering of the atmosphere: Normal lapse rate in Troposphere vs Inversion in Stratosphere.

The Five Thermal Atmospheric Zones

  • 1. Troposphere (0–18 km): Extends 8 km at poles, 18 km at equator. Contains 75% of atmospheric mass and all water vapor/dust. All weather phenomena (clouds, storms, rain) occur here. Temperature drops at Normal Lapse Rate: 6.5°C per 1,000 m (1°C per 165 m).
  • 2. Stratosphere (18–50 km): Temperature rises due to absorption of UV rays by the Ozone Layer (Ozonosphere, 15–35 km). Free from clouds and dust; ideal for flying jet aircraft. Boundary is Stratopause.
  • 3. Mesosphere (50–80 km): Temperature drops again with altitude, reaching -100°C at the Mesopause (coldest place in Earth's atmosphere). Meteors burning up due to friction are visible here as 'shooting stars'.
  • 4. Thermosphere / Ionosphere (80–400 km): Temperature rises dramatically above 1,000°C due to solar radiation. Contains electrically charged ions that reflect high-frequency radio waves back to Earth (D, E, F layers). Hosts the magnificent Auroras (Aurora Borealis in the Arctic, Aurora Australis in the Antarctic).
  • 5. Exosphere (400+ km): The outermost fringe gradually thinning into interplanetary vacuum. Dominated by ultra-light hydrogen and helium atoms.

Planetary Pressure Belts, Winds & Cyclones

Doldrums, Horse Latitudes, Roaring Forties, and World Local Winds

Equatorial Low (0°–5°)

The Doldrums (ITCZ)

Zone of intense solar heating. Air warms, expands, and rises vertically, creating an equatorial low-pressure trough. Characterized by calm air, absence of horizontal wind, and daily 4 o'clock convectional cloudbursts with thunder.

Sub-Tropical High (30°–35°)

The Horse Latitudes

Zone of descending dry air masses creating calm, stable high-pressure conditions. Sinking air produces clear skies and dry climate; world's great tropical deserts (Sahara, Thar, Atacama, Australian) are located in this latitude belt.

Westerlies (40°–60° S)

Roaring Forties & Fifties

In the Southern Hemisphere, unobstructed oceanic expanses allow Westerly winds to blow with stormy ferocious regularity:
• 40° S: Roaring Forties
• 50° S: Furious Fifties
• 60° S: Screaming Sixties.

Ocean Relief, Tides & The Global Ocean Currents

Mariana Trench, Saline Basins, Syzygy Tides, and the Warm & Cold Gyres

Global Ocean Gyres & Currents Zoom

Global Ocean Currents: Warm currents flow poleward; cold currents flow equatorward.

Ocean Relief & Salinity Highlights

  • Deepest Trenches: Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep, 11,034 m) in the Pacific; Puerto Rico Trench (8,605 m) in the Atlantic; Java / Sunda Trench (7,725 m) in the Indian Ocean.
  • Ocean Salinity: Average salinity is 35‰ (parts per thousand). Main salt is Sodium Chloride (77.8%), followed by Magnesium Chloride.
    • Highest Salinity: Lake Van (Turkey, 330‰), Dead Sea (238‰), Great Salt Lake (USA, 220‰).
  • Tidal Dynamics:
    • Spring Tides: Highest tides occurring on New Moon and Full Moon when Sun, Earth, Moon are in a straight line (Syzygy).
    • Neap Tides: Lowest tides occurring when Moon is at right angles to Sun (Quadrature).
    • Highest Tides in World: Bay of Fundy (Canada) with tidal range of 16–18 meters.
  • Sargasso Sea: An ice-free calm region in the North Atlantic enclosed by the clockwise gyre of the Gulf Stream, North Atlantic Drift, Canaries, and North Equatorial Currents. Unique sea with no land boundaries, densely covered by floating Sargassum seaweed.
