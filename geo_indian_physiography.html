India: Dimensions, Frontiers & Geographic Coordinates

Cardinal Coordinates, Coastline Extents, Tropic of Cancer, and Boundary Demarcations

Cardinal Points

Territorial Limits

• Latitudes: 8°4' N to 37°6' N (Mainland). Northernmost point is Indira Col (Ladakh); southernmost mainland point is Kanyakumari / Cape Comorin (TN); southernmost point of Indian Union is Indira Point / Pygmalion Point (6°45' N, Great Nicobar).
• Longitudes: 68°7' E (Ghuar Mota, Gujarat) to 97°25' E (Kibithu, Arunachal Pradesh).
• Distance: North to South = 3,214 km; East to West = 2,933 km. Total Area = 32,87,263 sq km (7th in world).

23°30' N Parallel

Tropic of Cancer (8 States)

The Tropic of Cancer divides India into almost two equal halves, passing through 8 States from West to East:
1. Gujarat
2. Rajasthan
3. Madhya Pradesh (longest stretch)
4. Chhattisgarh
5. Jharkhand (passes near Ranchi)
6. West Bengal
7. Tripura
8. Mizoram.

Land & Maritime Borders

International Frontiers

• Land Boundary: 15,200 km across 7 countries:
– Bangladesh: 4,096.7 km (Longest; Radcliffe Line)
– China: 3,488 km (McMahon Line 1914)
– Pakistan: 3,323 km (Radcliffe Line)
– Nepal: 1,751 km; Myanmar: 1,643 km; Bhutan: 699 km; Afghanistan: 106 km (Durand Line).
• Coastline: 7,516.6 km (Mainland 5,422.6 km + Islands 2,094 km).

The Northern Himalayas: Himadri, Himachal & Shiwaliks

Tectonic Origin from the Tethys Sea, World's Highest Summits, and Longitudinal Zones

The Great Himalayan Wall Zoom

Young fold mountains formed by the ongoing collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.

The Three Parallel Himalayan Belts

  • 1. Himadri (Greater / Inner Himalayas): Average elevation 6,000 meters; 2,400 km arc. Core composed of granite.
    • Highest summits: Mount Everest / Sagarmatha (8,848.86 m, Nepal), K2 / Godwin Austen (8,611 m, Karakoram - highest in Indian territory), Kanchenjunga (8,586 m, Sikkim - highest in undisputed India), Makalu (8,485 m), Dhaulagiri (8,167 m), Nanga Parbat (8,126 m), Annapurna (8,091 m), Nanda Devi (7,816 m, Uttarakhand).
  • 2. Himachal (Lesser / Middle Himalayas): Average elevation 3,700–4,500 m. Highly compressed metamorphic rocks.
    • Prominent ranges: Pir Panjal (longest and most important), Dhauladhar, Mussoorie range, Nag Tibba, Mahabharat range.
    • Famous valleys: Kashmir Valley (between Pir Panjal and Zaskar), Kangra Valley, Kullu Valley.
  • 3. Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas): Average elevation 900–1,100 m; width 10–50 km. Composed of unconsolidated river sediments and gravel. Longitudinal valleys lying between Lesser Himalayas and Shiwaliks are called Duns (e.g. Dehradun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun) in the west and Duars in the east.
  • Trans-Himalayas (Tibetan Zone): Lies north of Himadri. Includes Karakoram Range, Ladakh Range, and Zaskar Range. Hosts the world's longest non-polar glacier: Siachen Glacier (76 km) in the Nubra valley.

The Great Northern Plains: Morphological Belts

Bhabar, Tarai, Bhangar, and Khadar: The World's Largest Alluvial Aggradational Basin

1

Bhabar Belt

• 8–10 km narrow belt parallel to the Shiwalik foothills.
• Composed of coarse pebbles, boulders, and gravel dropped by mountain streams.
• High Porosity: Streams disappear underground into the gravel beds, leaving dry riverbeds on the surface. Unsuitable for agriculture.

2

Tarai Belt

• 10–20 km wide zone south of the Bhabar.
• Subterranean streams of Bhabar re-emerge at the surface, creating a wet, swampy, waterlogged marshland.
• Rich in fine silt; covered with dense forests and diverse wildlife (e.g. Jim Corbett, Dudhwa, Kaziranga). Widely cleared for sugarcane and rice farming.

3

Bhangar (Old Alluvium)

• Composed of older alluvium deposited in terraces well above the current flood limits of rivers.
• Dark in color; rich in humus but contains nodules of impure calcium carbonate called Kankar.
• Less fertile than Khadar; often requires intensive canal irrigation.

4

Khadar (New Alluvium)

• Composed of newer alluvium deposited annually by seasonal floodwaters along active river floodplains.
• Fine, clayey, fertile silt completely devoid of Kankar concretions.
• Most productive agricultural soil in India; supports multiple intensive cropping cycles per year.

The Peninsular Plateau & The Western Ghats (Sahyadris)

Ancient Gondwanaland Block, Chotanagpur's Riches, Anamudi, and the Coastal Kayals

Western Ghats (Sahyadris) Zoom

Western Ghats: Continuous 1,600 km mountain wall from Gujarat to Kanyakumari.

Peninsular Relief & Summits

  • Geological Antiquity: The Peninsular Plateau is a stable block of Archean gneisses and granites, forming part of ancient Gondwanaland. Never submerged under the sea since the Cambrian period.
  • Chotanagpur Plateau ('Ruhr of India'): Drained by the Damodar River; contains rich reserves of Gondwana coal, hematite iron ore, bauxite, and mica. Parasnath (1,365 m) is its highest peak (sacred Jain pilgrimage).
  • Western Ghats (Sahyadri): Continuous mountain wall (average height 1,200 m).
    • Highest peak in Peninsular / South India: Anamudi (2,695 m) in the Anaimalai Hills of Kerala.
    • Nilgiri Hills ('Blue Mountains'): Junction where Western and Eastern Ghats converge. Highest peak is Doddabetta (2,637 m).
  • Western Ghats Passes:
    • Thal Ghat: Connects Mumbai to Nashik / Kolkata.
    • Bhor Ghat: Connects Mumbai to Pune / Chennai.
    • Palghat (Palakkad Gap): Connects Kerala (Palakkad) to Tamil Nadu (Coimbatore).
  • Eastern Ghats: Discontinuous, heavily eroded relic hills dissected by Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi, and Cauvery. Highest peak is Jindhagada / Arma Konda (1,680 m) in Andhra Pradesh.

Coastal Plains & Island Water Channels

Konkan to Coromandel, Kerala Kayals, and the 10°, 9°, 8° Degree Channels

Western vs. Eastern Coastal Plains

  • • Western Coastal Plain: Narrow, submerged coast lying between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea. Subdivisions: Konkan Coast (Maharashtra/Goa), Kannad Coast (Karnataka), and Malabar Coast (Kerala). Dotted with natural harbors (Mumbai, Mormugao, Kochi) and lagoons called Kayals (e.g. Vembanad Lake). Rivers form estuaries, not deltas.
  • • Eastern Coastal Plain: Broad, emergent coast lying between Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal. Subdivisions: Northern Circars (between Mahanadi and Krishna) and Coromandel Coast (between Krishna and Kanyakumari). Rivers form expansive deltas (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery). Famous lagoons: Chilika Lake (Odisha - largest brackish water lake in India) and Pulicat Lake (Andhra/TN).

Strategic Maritime Channels

  • • Ten Degree Channel (10° N): Separates the Andaman Islands (Little Andaman) from the Nicobar Islands (Car Nicobar) in the Bay of Bengal.
  • • Nine Degree Channel (9° N): Separates the island of Minicoy from the main Lakshadweep archipelago.
  • • Eight Degree Channel (8° N): Separates the Indian island of Minicoy from the Republic of Maldives.
  • • Duncan Passage: Separates South Andaman from Little Andaman.
  • • Barren Island: India's and South Asia's only active volcano, located in the Andaman Sea.
  • • Palk Strait & Adam's Bridge (Ram Setu): Separates India (Tamil Nadu) from Sri Lanka.
