Established April 1, 1935 • Central banking authority, currency issuer, and lender of last resort

Central Banking Apex: Reserve Bank of India (Mumbai)

Established under the RBI Act 1934 on the recommendation of the Hilton Young Commission (1926). Nationalized on January 1, 1949.

Genesis & Commission

Recommended by Hilton Young Commission (1926). Commenced operations on April 1, 1935 with ₹5 crore share capital. Nationalized on Jan 1, 1949 under the Public Ownership Act.

Historical Governors

• 1st Governor: Sir Osborne Smith (1935–37).
• 1st Indian Governor: Sir C.D. Deshmukh (1943–49), who oversaw nationalization.
• Only PM to serve as Governor: Dr. Manmohan Singh (1982–85).

Currency Issuance

Sole authority to issue banknotes under Section 22 of RBI Act (Minimum Reserve System of ₹200 crore: ₹115 crore in gold + ₹85 crore in foreign securities). ₹1 notes and coins are issued by MoF.

Banker & Forex Custodian

Banker to Central & State Govts (manages WMA - Ways & Means Advances). Banker's Bank & Lender of Last Resort. Manages India's Foreign Exchange Reserves and regulates foreign exchange under FEMA 1999.

2. Measures of Money Supply in India (M0, M1, M2, M3, M4)

RBI Working Group classifications ranging from high-powered reserve money to broad money

Liquidity Spectrum
M0 / Reserve Money High-Powered
Monetary Base

Currency in Circulation + Bankers' Deposits with the RBI + 'Other' Deposits with the RBI. Base on which the commercial banking system creates credit.

M1 / Narrow Money Most Liquid
Transactions Money

Currency held by public + Demand Deposits (savings/current accounts) with commercial & cooperative banks + Other deposits with RBI. Instant liquidity.

M2 M1 + Post Office
Semi-Liquid

M1 + Post Office Savings Bank Deposits. Includes small rural savings held in postal banks.

M3 / Broad Money Standard Measure
Broad Money Aggregate

M1 + Time Deposits (Fixed/Recurring deposits) with the banking system. Most widely used measure by RBI for monetary policy formulation.

3. Quantitative & Qualitative Monetary Policy Instruments

Controlling liquidity, interest rates, and inflation through the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)

Policy Corridor
Instrument Nature / Mechanism Impact of Rate Increase on Economy Key Legal / Operational Feature
Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) Percentage of Net Demand & Time Liabilities (NDTL) banks must park as cash with RBI Sucks cash out of banking system → reduces loan supply → checks inflation Section 42 of RBI Act 1934; banks earn ZERO interest
Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) Percentage of NDTL banks must hold with themselves in liquid form (cash, gold, approved G-Secs) Forces banks to buy government bonds → restricts commercial credit Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act 1949
Repo Rate (Key Policy Rate) Rate at which RBI lends short-term liquidity to banks against collateral of government securities under LAF Increases cost of borrowing for banks → increases lending rates for public → cools demand Decided bimonthly by the 6-member MPC
Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) Collateral-free overnight deposit facility for banks to park surplus funds with RBI Absorbs surplus liquidity without requiring RBI to provide G-secs as collateral Introduced in April 2022; acts as floor of LAF corridor
Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Penal overnight window for banks to borrow funds in emergency by dipping into their statutory SLR quota Acts as upper ceiling of policy rate corridor (Repo + 25 bps) Introduced in 2011 to handle severe overnight volatility
Open Market Operations (OMO) Outright purchase or sale of government securities in open market by RBI Sale of G-Secs absorbs liquidity; Purchase of G-Secs injects liquidity into system Direct durable liquidity adjustment

The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) & Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT)

Constituted under Section 45ZB of the amended RBI Act 1934 in 2016. Comprises 6 members: 3 from RBI (Governor, Deputy Governor, Executive Director) and 3 external economists appointed by the Central Government. The RBI Governor has a casting vote in case of a tie. Statutory mandate is Flexible Inflation Targeting: maintaining headline CPI inflation at 4% with a tolerance band of +/- 2% (2% to 6%).

4. Commercial Banking, Nationalization & NPA Resolution

Historical milestones, Priority Sector Lending (PSL), and the Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC)

Banking Sector
1969 & 1980 Indira Gandhi Era

Bank Nationalization

• July 19, 1969: 14 major commercial banks with deposits >₹50 crore nationalized to break industrial monopoly and expand rural branches.
• April 15, 1980: 6 more commercial banks with deposits >₹200 crore nationalized. Expanded banking outreach across rural India.

40% Target Mandatory Quota

Priority Sector Lending (PSL)

Domestic commercial banks must allocate 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors: Agriculture (18%), Micro Enterprises (7.5%), Weaker Sections (12%), Education, Housing, Social Infrastructure, and Renewable Energy.

90-Day Norm Prudential Norms

NPA Classification & IBC 2016

Loans overdue >90 days classified as NPAs: Substandard (≤12 months), Doubtful (>12 months), and Loss Assets. Resolved through SARFAESI Act 2002, the landmark Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code (IBC) 2016, and NARCL (Bad Bank).
