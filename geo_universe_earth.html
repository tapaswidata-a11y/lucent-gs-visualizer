The Universe, Big Bang Theory & Cosmic Units

Cosmological Origins, Galactic Architecture, and Measuring Distances in Deep Space

1

The Big Bang Theory

Formulated by Georges Lemaître (1927). All matter was concentrated into a tiny primeval point with infinite density and temperature, which exploded ~13.8 billion years ago. Edwin Hubble established that galaxies are moving away from each other (expanding universe).

2

Galaxies & The Milky Way

Our solar system is in the Milky Way Galaxy (Akash Ganga), a barred spiral galaxy with ~100–400 billion stars. The closest major galaxy to us is the Andromeda Galaxy (M31), located 2.5 million light years away.

3

Cosmic Distance Units

• Astronomical Unit (AU): Mean Earth-Sun distance = 149.6 million km.
• Light Year (ly): Distance light travels in 1 year = 9.46 × 10¹² km.
• Parsec: Parallax second = 3.26 light years (largest unit of distance).

The Solar System: The Sun and 8 Planets

Terrestrial vs Jovian Planets, Physical Attributes, and Orbit Mechanics

NASA Solar System Graphic Zoom

Solar System: 8 major planets organized into Inner Terrestrial and Outer Jovian worlds.

The Sun: Central Luminary

  • • Composition: 71% Hydrogen, 26.5% Helium, 2.5% other elements. Energy generated by nuclear fusion (Hydrogen into Helium).
  • • Temperatures: Core temperature is 15 million K; surface (photosphere) temperature is ~6,000 K.
  • • Atmosphere: Chromosphere (thin layer of gas above photosphere) and Corona (outermost incandescent halo visible during a total solar eclipse).
  • • Sunspots: Cooler regions on the photosphere (~4,000 K) with intense magnetic fields following an 11-year cycle.
  • • Light Travel Time: Sunlight takes approximately 8 minutes and 20 seconds (500 seconds) to reach the Earth.

The Earth: Shape, Dimensions & Planetary Motions

The Geoid Spheroid, Rotation, Revolution, Solstices, and Equinoxes

The Blue Marble • Apollo 17 Zoom

Earth's true shape is a Geoid (oblate spheroid flattened at the poles, bulging at the equator).

Rotation vs. Revolution

  • Shape & Size: Equatorial diameter = 12,756 km; Polar diameter = 12,714 km (difference of 42 km). Equatorial circumference = 40,075 km.
  • Rotation: Earth spins on its axis tilted at 23.5° (making 66.5° with the orbital plane) from West to East once every 23 hours, 56 minutes, 4 seconds.
    Effects: Day and night, Coriolis force (deflection of winds/currents), rise and fall of diurnal ocean tides.
  • Revolution: Earth revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit in 365 days, 5 hours, 48 minutes, 46 seconds (~365.25 days).
    Effects: Change of seasons, varying lengths of day and night throughout the year.
  • Perihelion & Aphelion:
    • Perihelion (Jan 3): Earth is closest to Sun (147 million km).
    • Aphelion (July 4): Earth is farthest from Sun (152 million km).

Latitudes, Longitudes & World Time Zones

The Coordinate Grid, Prime Meridian, Indian Standard Time, and the Date Line

Parallels of Latitude

Angular Distance from Equator

• Equator (0°): The only Great Circle parallel.
• Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N) & Capricorn (23.5° S)
• Arctic (66.5° N) & Antarctic (66.5° S) Circles
• Linear distance between 1° of latitude is approximately 111 km everywhere on Earth.

Meridians of Longitude

Time and Longitude Relationship

• Prime Meridian (0°): Passes through the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, London.
• Total 360 meridians. 360° = 24 hours.
• 15° Longitude = 1 Hour
• 1° Longitude = 4 Minutes
• Places East of Greenwich gain time (ahead of GMT); places West lose time (behind GMT).

IST & IDL

Indian Standard Time & Date Line

• Indian Standard Time (IST): Calculated from 82.5° E (82°30' E) meridian at Mirzapur, UP. IST is GMT +5 hours 30 minutes.
• International Date Line (IDL): Follows 180° meridian. Crossing West adds a day (+1 calendar date); crossing East subtracts a day. Zig-zags at Bering Strait and Pacific islands.

Internal Structure of the Earth & Discontinuities

Chemical Composition, Rheological Layers, and Landmark Seismic Boundaries

Crust, Mantle, Core Differentiation Zoom

Concentric density layers: SIAL (crust), SIMA (crust/mantle), and NIFE (core).

The Three Concentric Shells

  • 1. Crust (Lithosphere): Outermost solid rind (0.5% of Earth volume).
    • Continental Crust: 30–50 km thick, lighter granitic rocks, rich in SIAL (Silica & Aluminium), density 2.7 g/cm³.
    • Oceanic Crust: 5–10 km thick, denser basaltic rocks, rich in SIMA (Silica & Magnesium), density 3.0 g/cm³.
  • 2. Mantle (Mesosphere): 2,900 km thick (occupies 83% of Earth volume and 67% mass). Composed of dense silicate rocks (olivine, pyroxene). The upper portion (up to 400 km) is the Asthenosphere—plastic, semi-molten layer on which tectonic plates float.
  • 3. Core (Barysphere / Centrosphere): From 2,900 km to center (6,371 km). Composed of NIFE (Nickel & Iron).
    • Outer Core: Liquid state (prevents S-waves from passing). Convection generates Earth’s magnetic field.
    • Inner Core: Solid state due to tremendous pressure, temperature ~6,000°C, density up to 13 g/cm³.
