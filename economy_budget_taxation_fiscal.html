Constitutional Framework & Budgetary Passage

Articles 112 to 117 governing parliamentary financial procedures

Article 112: Annual Financial Statement

The word "Budget" is nowhere mentioned in the Constitution of India. Article 112 mandates the President to cause to be laid before both Houses of Parliament an "Annual Financial Statement" showing estimated receipts and expenditures for the financial year (1st April to 31st March).

Article 266: Government Funds

Consolidated Fund of India (Art 266(1)): All revenues received, loans raised, and loan recoveries flow here. No money can be withdrawn without parliamentary appropriation.
Public Account of India (Art 266(2)): Bank deposits, provident funds, judicial deposits held in trust. No parliamentary sanction needed for payments.

Article 267: Contingency Fund

An imprest placed at the disposal of the President of India to meet urgent, unforeseen expenditures pending parliamentary authorization. Administered by the Finance Secretary on behalf of the President (corpus enhanced to ₹30,000 Crore in Finance Act 2021).

Six Sequential Stages of Budget Passage in Parliament

STAGE 01
Presentation

Budget Speech on 1st February by Finance Minister; no discussion occurs on presentation day.

STAGE 02
General Discussion

General principles discussed in both Houses for 3-4 days; no cut motions or voting occurs.

STAGE 03
Scrutiny by DRSCs

Houses adjourn for 3-4 weeks. 24 Departmentally Related Standing Committees examine demands.

STAGE 04
Voting on Demands

Exclusive to Lok Sabha (Art 113). Cut Motions (Policy, Economy, Token) moved. Ends with Guillotine.

STAGE 05
Appropriation Bill

Article 114: Authorizes withdrawal of funds from Consolidated Fund of India for voted + charged expenditures.

STAGE 06
Finance Bill

Enacts legal taxation proposals. Governed by Provisional Collection of Taxes Act 1931 (passed in 75 days).

Revenue vs Capital Budget Architecture

Classification of receipts and disbursements based on liability and asset impact

Revenue Account (No Asset/Liability Impact)

Operational Flow
1. Revenue Receipts: Receipts that neither create liability nor reduce government assets.
  • Tax Revenue: Direct Taxes (Income Tax, Corporation Tax) and Indirect Taxes (GST, Customs, Central Excise on Petroleum).
  • Non-Tax Revenue: Dividends & profits from PSUs/RBI, Interest receipts from states, Fees, Fines, Spectrum auction proceeds.
2. Revenue Expenditure: Consumptive spending that neither creates physical/financial assets nor reduces liabilities.
  • Interest payments on sovereign debts (single largest component ~20% of budget).
  • Defense revenue expenditure (salaries, pensions, operations).
  • Subsidies (Food, Fertilizer, Petroleum), administrative salaries, and pensions.
  • Grants given to State Governments (even if states use them for asset creation!).

Capital Account (Asset/Liability Impacting)

Asset Creation / Debt
1. Capital Receipts: Receipts that either create a sovereign liability or reduce government assets.
  • Debt Receipts: Market borrowings (G-Secs, T-Bills), External commercial loans, Sovereign bonds (creates future repayment liability).
  • Non-Debt Capital Receipts (NDCR): Recovery of loans from State governments, Disinvestment / privatization proceeds (DIPAM).
2. Capital Expenditure (Capex): Productive outlay resulting in physical/financial asset creation or debt reduction.
  • Construction of national highways, railways, ports, power stations.
  • Capital defense acquisitions (fighter jets, naval warships, weaponry).
  • Loans and advances given to State Governments & Union Territories.
  • Repayment of past sovereign debt borrowings.

Government Deficits & Quantitative Formulas

Critical mathematical formulations frequently examined in Prelims & Mains

Revenue Deficit (RD)
RD = Revenue Exp − Revenue Receipts

Reflects government's inability to finance ordinary day-to-day governance through its routine tax and non-tax revenues. Results in dissavings.

Effective Revenue Deficit
ERD = RD − Grants for Capital Assets

Introduced in Union Budget 2011-12. Deducts grants given to states for capital asset creation (e.g. PMGSY roads) from Revenue Deficit.

Fiscal Deficit (FD)
FD = Total Exp − (Rev Rec + Non-Debt Cap Rec)

Exact measure of government's total borrowing requirements from domestic and external sources. Equals Total Net Borrowings.

Primary Deficit (PD)
PD = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments

Reflects borrowing requirement exclusively to finance current expenditures, excluding obligations inherited from past loans.

FRBM Act 2003 & N.K. Singh Review Committee

Enacted under the Vajpayee Government to institutionalize financial discipline. Mandated eliminating Revenue Deficit and restricting Fiscal Deficit to 3% of GDP. In 2016, the N.K. Singh Committee recommended targeting Debt-to-GDP ratio as the primary fiscal anchor (60% combined: 40% Centre, 20% States) with an "escape clause" of 0.5% during national security, war, or calamity triggers.

Indian Taxation Architecture & Goods and Services Tax (GST)

Direct vs Indirect Taxes, 101st Constitutional Amendment Act & the GST Council

Direct Taxes (CBDT)

Taxes where incidence and impact fall on the same entity (cannot be shifted). Progressive in nature.

  • Corporation Tax: Tax on net income/profit of companies.
  • Personal Income Tax: Progressive rates on individual earnings.
  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT): Tax on equity trading transactions.

Indirect Taxes (CBIC)

Taxes where incidence is on consumer but collected by seller (burden shifted). Regressive in nature.

  • GST: Unified destination-based consumption tax.
  • Customs Duty: Tax on cross-border imports/exports.
  • Excise on Non-GST items: Petroleum, crude, ATF, natural gas, alcohol for human consumption.

Cess vs Surcharge Distinction

Cess: Tax on tax levied for a specific earmarked purpose (e.g. Health & Education Cess 4%). Proceeds cannot be diverted and are NOT shared with states under the divisible pool.

Surcharge: Additional tax on tax levied without an earmarked purpose (flows directly into Consolidated Fund, not shared with states).

The Goods & Services Tax (GST) Architecture

Constitutional Amendments

101st Amendment Act 2016: Inserted Art 246A (concurrent power to tax goods & services), Art 269A (IGST collection by Centre & apportioned), and Art 279A (GST Council).

Structure of GST

Dual GST Model (inspired by Canada). Intra-state sales attract equal CGST + SGST. Inter-state sales attract IGST (Integrated GST). Four standard slabs: 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%.

GST Council Voting Power

Chaired by Union Finance Minister. Union Govt has 1/3rd (33.3%) voting weight; all State Finance Ministers combined hold 2/3rds (66.7%). Decision requires 3/4th (75%) majority.

Goods Outside GST

Alcohol for human consumption (State Excise + VAT). Five petroleum products (petrol, diesel, crude, natural gas, ATF) deferred until recommended by GST Council. Electricity & stamp duty stay under states.

Finance Commission of India (Article 280)

Quasi-judicial constitutional balancing wheel of fiscal federalism

Mandate & Composition

  • Constitutional Basis: Article 280 mandates President to constitute a Finance Commission every 5th year or earlier.
  • Composition: Chairman + 4 other members appointed by the President. Qualifications determined by Parliament under Finance Commission Act 1951.
  • Core Recommendations:
    • Vertical Devolution: Division of divisible net proceeds between Union and States.
    • Horizontal Devolution: Allocation between states based on equity and efficiency criteria.
    • Grants-in-aid to States out of Consolidated Fund under Article 275.
    • Measures to augment State Consolidated Funds to supplement PRIs and ULBs.

15th Finance Commission (N.K. Singh) Devolution Weights

Criteria Weight (%) Rationale & Measurement
Income Distance 45.0% Distance from highest GSDP per capita state (Haryana); ensures fiscal equalization.
Population (2011 Census) 15.0% Entirely based on 2011 census, replacing the dual 1971/2011 formula of 14th FC.
Area 15.0% Compensates larger geographical states for higher administration and infrastructure costs.
Forest & Ecology 10.0% Dense and moderately dense forest cover; incentivizes environmental conservation.
Demographic Performance 12.5% Rewards states with lower Total Fertility Rates (TFR) to address southern state concerns.
Tax & Fiscal Effort 2.5% Rewards higher tax collection efficiency relative to GSDP capacity.
