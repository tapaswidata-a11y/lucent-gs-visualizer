Cell Organelles & Molecular Genetics

Eukaryotic subcellular architecture, ATP synthesis, and DNA double helix

Mitochondria
"Powerhouse of the Cell"

Site of aerobic ATP synthesis via Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation. Uniquely possesses its own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes (semi-autonomous endosymbiont).

Lysosomes
"Suicidal Bags of the Cell"

Discovered by Christian de Duve; packed with acid hydrolase digestive enzymes. In injured or necrotic cells, lysosomes rupture to self-digest and clear cellular waste.

Ribosomes
"Protein Factories"

Smallest organelle, non-membrane bound ribonucleoprotein granules (Palade particles). Translates mRNA into polypeptide proteins (80S in eukaryotes, 70S in prokaryotes).

DNA Double Helix
Watson & Crick Model (1953)

Antiparallel double helix. Purines pair with pyrimidines: Adenine = Thymine (2 H-bonds); Guanine ≡ Cytosine (3 H-bonds). In RNA, Uracil (U) replaces Thymine.

Human Digestion & Renal Excretion Architecture

Enzymatic hydrolysis, liver functions, and nephron urine formation

Digestive Glands & Enzymes

Salivary Amylase (Ptyalin): In mouth; hydrolyzes starch into maltose disaccharide (optimal pH 6.8).
Stomach (Gastric Juice): Secretes HCl (kills pathogens, creates acidic pH 1.5–2.0) and Pepsin (cleaves proteins into peptones/proteoses). In infants, Rennin curdles milk protein casein.
The Liver (Largest Gland ~1.5 kg): Synthesizes Bile (stored in gallbladder). Bile contains no digestive enzymes but contains bile salts that emulsify dietary fats.
Pancreas: Complete digestive juice containing Trypsin (proteins), Amylase (starches), and Lipase (emulsified lipids).

Renal Excretion & The Nephron

  • Nephron: Structural and functional microscopic filtration unit of the kidney (~1 million per kidney). Consists of Bowman's capsule, Glomerulus, Proximal & Distal tubules, and Loop of Henle.
  • Ultrafiltration: Blood filtered under high pressure across glomerular capillaries; produces ~180 Litres of glomerular filtrate daily, of which 99% is reabsorbed.
  • Urine Characteristics: Pale yellow coloration is caused by the pigment Urochrome (end-product of hemoglobin breakdown). Slightly acidic (average pH ~6.0).
  • Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi): Chemically crystallized Calcium Oxalate (CaC₂O₄).

Circulatory Dynamics, Blood Groups & Heart Architecture

Erythrocyte lifespan, ABO & Rh compatibility, and cardiac pacemaker mechanics

Formed Blood Elements

  • RBCs (Erythrocytes): Biconcave, enucleated (no nucleus in mature human RBCs; camel & llama have nucleated RBCs). Lifespan ~120 days. Destroyed in the Spleen ("graveyard of RBCs"). Packed with iron-rich Hemoglobin.
  • WBCs (Leukocytes): Nucleated immune sentinels. Granulocytes (Neutrophils - phagocytic) and Agranulocytes (Lymphocytes - T & B cells producing antibodies; Monocytes - largest WBC).
  • Platelets (Thrombocytes): Formed from megakaryocytes in bone marrow; initiate coagulation. Require Vitamin K and Ca²⁺.

ABO & Rh Blood Groups

Discovered by Karl Landsteiner (1900). Governed by antigens on RBCs and antibodies in plasma:

Universal Donor: O Rh-negative (O−)

RBCs possess neither A, B, nor Rh antigens; cannot trigger recipient antibody attack.

Universal Recipient: AB Rh-positive (AB+)

Plasma has no anti-A, anti-B, or anti-Rh antibodies.

Cardiac Cycle & Pressure

  • Cardiac Pacemaker: The Sinoatrial (SA) Node in right atrium generates spontaneous rhythmic impulses (72 beats/minute).
  • Atrioventricular (AV) Node: Relays impulses down the Bundle of His and Purkinje fibers.
  • Blood Pressure: Measured using a Sphygmomanometer on brachial artery. Normal value is 120 / 80 mmHg (Systolic / Diastolic).
  • Heart Valves: Tricuspid (right atrium-ventricle), Bicuspid/Mitral (left atrium-ventricle), Semilunar (aorta and pulmonary artery).

Nervous Coordination & Endocrine Hormone Architecture

Cerebral functional zones, Pituitary master control, and Pancreatic insulin

Brain Anatomy & Control Centers

Cerebrum (Forebrain): Largest portion of human brain (~80%); center of intelligence, memory, reasoning, language, and voluntary motor activities.
Cerebellum (Hindbrain): Coordinates voluntary muscular contractions and maintains body balance, posture, and equilibrium. Directly impaired by alcohol intoxication.
Medulla Oblongata: Controls involuntary autonomic functions: heartbeat, arterial blood pressure, respiratory rhythm, swallowing, coughing, and vomiting reflexes.
Hypothalamus: Regulates body temperature (thermostat of body), hunger, thirst, sleep, and endocrine master control via pituitary.

Endocrine Glands & Major Hormones

Pituitary ("Master Gland"): Growth hormone (GH), Oxytocin (milk ejection, labor contractions), ADH / Vasopressin (water reabsorption).
Diabetes Mellitus vs Insipidus:

• Diabetes Mellitus: Insulin deficiency from Pancreatic β-cells (high blood glucose).
• Diabetes Insipidus: ADH/Vasopressin deficiency from Pituitary (excess diluted urine without glucose).

Thyroid Gland: Secretes Thyroxine (regulates basal metabolic rate BMR; requires Iodine; deficiency causes Goiter).
Adrenal Glands: Medulla secretes Adrenaline (Epinephrine), the "Emergency / 3F (Fight, Flight, Fright) Hormone".

Human Diseases & Diagnostic Tests

Bacterial Infections: Tuberculosis (BCG vaccine), Cholera (Vibrio cholerae), Typhoid (Salmonella; diagnosed via Widal Test), Tetanus, Diphtheria, Pertussis (DPT triple antigen vaccine).
Viral Infections: Polio (Salk injectable, Sabin oral), Rabies (Hydrophobia), Dengue & Chikungunya (Aedes aegypti vector), AIDS (HIV retrovirus; diagnosed via ELISA and Western Blot).
Protozoan Infections:

• Malaria: Plasmodium vivax / falciparum transmitted by female Anopheles mosquito (treated with Quinine from Cinchona bark).
• Kala-Azar (Leishmaniasis): Transmitted by Phlebotomus sandfly vector.
